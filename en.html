Why SmartKidsMontis?

Three good reasons why we act together as parents

📊

The Reality

More and more primary school children own a smartphone — often from age 8 or 9. Studies show that early smartphone use impairs concentration, sleep, and social development. The brain doesn't finish developing until the mid-twenties.

❓

The Questions

"Everyone else has one!" — Children face enormous peer pressure. Parents feel alone with the decision and uncertain: Is it okay to say no? What if my child is excluded?

🤝

The Opportunity

Together it's easier! When many parents in a class agree, peer pressure disappears. Children can be children — and we parents support each other in making conscious media decisions.

Resources & Support

Background knowledge, tips, and support for parents

Scientific findings on smartphone use by children:

  • The WHO recommends strictly limiting screen time for children under 5.
  • Studies link early smartphone use to concentration problems, sleep disorders, and anxiety.
  • The brain develops into young adulthood — the prefrontal cortex (impulse control) doesn't fully mature until around age 25.
  • The BLIKK study (2017) confirms: Excessive media use leads to developmental issues in children.

Experts and educators recommend:

  • No personal smartphone before secondary school (many recommend: not before 14).
  • Establish shared media rules within the family.
  • Teach media literacy before giving a personal device.
  • Have open conversations about online risks — without fearmongering.

Good alternatives when reachability matters:

  • Kids' phone: Simple phones without internet (e.g. Nokia 3310, Emporia).
  • Kids' smartwatch: GPS tracking and calls without social media (e.g. Xplora, Anio).
  • Family devices: A shared tablet at home, managed by parents.
  • Prepaid without data: SIM card for calls and texts only.
  • Talk to other parents in your child's class — it's easier together.
  • Explain to your child why you're waiting — not as a punishment, but out of care.
  • Be a role model with your own smartphone use.
  • Create alternatives: playdates, hobbies, quality time together.
  • Agree on a "media contract" with clear rules when the time comes.

When things get difficult:

  • Your child feels left out? Talk to the parents of their friends.
  • Group chats becoming a problem? Inform the class teacher.
  • Cyberbullying? Contact the school counselor or a helpline.
  • Need someone to talk to? Write to us — we're happy to help.

About Us & Contact

Our Story

SmartKidsMontis started in spring 2025 from conversations between parents at Montessori School Inning am Ammersee. We realized: many of us share the same concerns about smartphones and children — and the same uncertainty.

From these conversations grew an initiative. Not from the school, but from parents for parents. Our goal: to create an environment together where children can grow up without smartphone pressure.

We are not experts and not opponents of technology. We are parents who want to make conscious decisions — and who know that it's easier together.
